# Shenzhen for Westerners / 深圳指南

Shenzhen (深圳, Shēnzhèn) is the mainland Chinese megacity on Hong Kong's northern border: China's hardware and
internet capital, younger and more international than most of China, yet with far less English than Hong Kong.
This skill is a briefing for visitors, business travellers and people relocating from Europe, North America or
Australia. Figures were checked in October 2026 and carry their date.

## Orientation
- **Scale.** 18.25 million permanent residents at end-2025 (official bulletin, May 2026), the fastest-growing big
  city in China; GDP about ¥3.9 trillion in 2025 (roughly US$540bn), third on the mainland after Shanghai and
  Beijing; area 1,997 km², nine districts plus the Dapeng New District.
- **Story.** A rural county (Bao'an) of fishing and farming villages until the city was founded in 1979 and the
  Special Economic Zone (SEZ) opened in August 1980 as Deng Xiaoping's test bed for market reforms; Deng's 1992
  "Southern Tour" speech here restarted reform after 1989. "Shenzhen speed" (one floor every three days on the
  Guomao building, 1984) became the national slogan. Nearly everyone is a migrant ("来了就是深圳人": once here,
  you're a Shenzhener), which is why the city feels open and why **Mandarin**, not Cantonese, is the street language.
- **Districts** (west to east):
  - *Nanshan* 南山: tech (Tencent, DJI, Science & Technology Park 科技园), universities, Shenzhen Bay; **Shekou** 蛇口
    at its tip is the historic expat enclave (Sea World 海上世界, international schools, ferry port).
  - *Futian* 福田: central business district, city government, Lianhuashan Park, the Futian border checkpoint and the
    Futian high-speed rail station under the CBD.
  - *Luohu* 罗湖: the old downtown, Dongmen shopping streets, Lo Wu/Luohu border crossing and Shenzhen railway station.
  - *Bao'an* 宝安: the airport (SZX) and the factory belt; *Longhua* 龙华: Shenzhen North HSR station, Foxconn;
    *Longgang* 龙岗: Huawei's Bantian 坂田 campus, Dafen painters' village; *Yantian* 盐田: container port, Dameisha
    beach; *Pingshan* 坪山: BYD; *Guangming* 光明: science city; *Dapeng* 大鹏: beaches and the Ming-dynasty fortress.
- **Climate.** Subtropical: hot and humid May–October (30–35 °C), typhoon season June–October, pleasant
  November–April, winter 10–20 °C with no indoor heating. Typhoon warnings are colour-coded (blue, yellow, orange, red);
  at red, workplaces and schools close and the metro may stop. Tap water is not drunk unboiled.

## Entering China and crossing from Hong Kong
- **Visas (changes often; verify at nia.gov.cn or the Chinese embassy).** As of October 2026: unilateral 30-day
  visa-free entry for ordinary passports of most EU states, Switzerland, Norway, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South
  Korea and others; **the UK and Canada** got 30 days from 17 February 2026 (announced through 31 December 2026).
  **US citizens are not on that list** and use the **240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit**: enter at a designated
  port with a confirmed onward ticket to a *different* country or region within 240 hours; Hong Kong and Macau count
  as different regions, so Hong Kong → Shenzhen → anywhere other than Hong Kong qualifies (a Hong Kong round trip does
  not). Shenzhen's airport and land/sea ports are designated ports and the 240-hour rules allow travel across 24
  provinces; check the current port and province list. Otherwise apply for an L (tourist) or M (business) visa at
  a visa centre before travelling; there is no visa on arrival. Hotels register foreigners with the police automatically; in a
  private home you must register at the local police station within 24 hours.
- **From Hong Kong** (hours checked October 2026; confirm before a late trip):
  - **Lo Wu / Luohu** 罗湖: MTR East Rail to Lo Wu, walk across; 06:30–24:00. The classic, busiest crossing.
  - **Lok Ma Chau / Futian Checkpoint** 福田口岸: East Rail to Lok Ma Chau; 06:30–22:30; lands you on Metro Line 4/10.
  - **High-speed rail** from West Kowloon to Futian (about 14 min) or Shenzhen North (about 19 min); immigration
    for both sides is done in West Kowloon; station open 06:30–23:30; tickets on 12306 or Trip.com with passport.
  - **Shenzhen Bay** 深圳湾口岸: by bus or car over the bridge from the New Territories; the usual choice for
    Shekou/Nanshan; 06:30–24:00.
  - **Huanggang** 皇岗: rebuilt as a passenger-only port and, per reports in September 2026, the only crossing open
    **24 hours** — verify the current state. **Liantang / Heung Yuen Wai** 莲塘 serves the east.
  - **Ferry**: Shekou Cruise Center ↔ Hong Kong Macau Ferry Terminal, Hong Kong airport SkyPier (no HK immigration
    when transferring), and Macau; about 30–60 min.
- **Shenzhen Bao'an airport (SZX)** 深圳宝安机场: Metro Line 11 to Futian/Che Kung Miao in about 35 min; mostly
  domestic and Asian routes, growing long-haul; Hong Kong airport is still the main intercontinental gateway.

## Money
- Currency is the renminbi (RMB, ¥, CNY); Hong Kong dollars are not accepted. No tipping anywhere.
- **Mobile payment is universal**: Alipay 支付宝 and WeChat Pay 微信支付 accept foreign Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB
  and Discover linked in the app, no Chinese bank account needed. Set both up **before arrival** (passport photo page
  and a phone number that can receive SMS). Typical terms (verify): no fee under ¥200 per payment, 3 % above; limits
  around ¥5,000 per payment and ¥50,000 per year on a foreign card; no sending money to individuals or red packets.
- Scan-to-pay works for taxis, metro (QR in the Alipay "Transport" mini-program), street stalls, vending machines
  and temples. Foreign credit cards work in hotels, big malls and some chains only. Cash is legal tender and
  refusing it is penalised (rules tightened in 2026), but keep small notes; ATMs of Bank of China / ICBC take foreign cards.
- Receipts for expenses are **fapiao** 发票, a tax invoice you must ask for at payment; most merchants now issue them
  electronically to a WeChat/Alipay "invoice title".

## Getting online and the apps that matter
- **The Great Firewall** blocks Google (search, Gmail, Maps, Drive), WhatsApp, Signal, Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube,
  Wikipedia, many Western news sites and most Western AI services. Install and test a **VPN before arrival**; VPN use
  by individuals is tolerated, selling them is not. **International roaming or a travel eSIM** (Airalo, Holafly, a
  Hong Kong SIM such as China Mobile HK) routes through the home network and is *not* filtered — the easiest option
  for a short trip. A local SIM (China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom; passport required) is filtered.
- **Must-have apps**: WeChat 微信 (messaging, payments, mini-programs, business contact — the local business card),
  Alipay 支付宝 (payments; inside it DiDi, metro codes, bike share, Meituan, train tickets, translation), Amap 高德地图
  (navigation, has an English mode; Apple Maps also works, Google Maps is unreliable), DiDi 滴滴 (ride hailing with an
  English interface; Uber does not operate), Meituan 美团 / Ele.me 饿了么 (food delivery, in Chinese), Dianping 大众点评
  (restaurant reviews), Trip.com (hotels, trains, flights in English), 12306 (official train tickets), Baidu Translate
  or WeChat's built-in translation (camera translation for menus).
- Hotels: only hotels licensed for foreigners can check you in — filter for "foreigners accepted" on Trip.com;
  international chains and anything 4-star+ are fine.

## Getting around
- **Metro** 地铁: 17 lines and about 640 km (mid-2026), among the largest networks in the world, clean, signed in
  English, 06:30–23:30, ¥2–14. Pay with the Alipay/WeChat transport QR, a Shenzhen Tong 深圳通 card, or contactless
  on some gates. Line 1 (Luohu–airport corridor), Line 2 (Shekou, Sea World), Line 11 (airport express), Line 14
  (east). Rush hours 08:00–09:30 and 18:00–19:30 are crowded.
- **Taxis** are all electric (mostly BYD), metered, start about ¥10; drivers rarely speak English — show the
  destination in Chinese. DiDi is cheaper and lets you type the address in English.
- **Intercity**: high-speed rail from Shenzhen North 深圳北 / Futian 福田 to Guangzhou South (about 30–40 min),
  Hong Kong (14–19 min), Xiamen, Changsha, Shanghai (under 8 h); book with passport on 12306/Trip.com. The
  Shenzhen–Zhongshan Link (opened June 2024) puts the west bank of the Pearl River 30 min away by car.
- Shared bikes (Meituan, HelloBike via Alipay) and e-scooters are everywhere; e-bikes use the pavements, look both ways.

## The tech and business scene
- **Why it matters.** The deepest hardware supply chain in the world: a prototype can be designed, sourced, built and
  shipped within the city. R&D is over 5 % of GDP and Shenzhen files more international (PCT) patents than any
  other Chinese city; it hosts the Shenzhen Stock Exchange and its ChiNext growth board.
- **Companies headquartered here** (founding year, founder): Huawei (1987, Ren Zhengfei; Bantian campus, Longgang;
  the "Ox Horn" European-style campus is in Dongguan), Tencent (1998, Ma Huateng "Pony Ma"; WeChat), BYD (1995, Wang
  Chuanfu; EVs and batteries, Pingshan), DJI (2006, Frank Wang; drones), Ping An (1988; insurance and finance), China
  Merchants Bank (1987), ZTE (1985), SF Express (1993, Wang Wei; logistics), Mindray (medical devices), BGI (genomics),
  UBTech (robots), Vanke (property), Transsion (phones for Africa), Insta360, Honor. Many "Shenzhen"
  factories are in neighbouring Dongguan and Huizhou.
- **Places to go**: Huaqiangbei 华强北 (the world's largest electronics market: components, phones, repair, SEG Plaza;
  haggling expected, quality varies), Nanshan Science & Technology Park and Tencent's Binhai towers, Qianhai 前海
  (the Shenzhen–Hong Kong finance and services zone, with Hong Kong-style legal and tax arrangements), OCT-Loft 华侨城
  创意园 (design studios), Shenzhen Software Park, Hong Kong–Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park at the border.
  Trade fairs: China Hi-Tech Fair (November), ITES Shenzhen industrial fair (spring), Global Sources shows in Hong Kong (April,
  October). Sourcing: suppliers list on 1688 (Chinese) and Alibaba; expect minimum order quantities, ask for samples,
  and visit the factory.
- **Business customs**: exchange **WeChat** first, business cards second (give and take with both hands, read it);
  titles and seniority matter; meetings start with small talk and tea; a direct "no" is rare — "we'll study it"
  often means no; decisions come from the top after the meeting; dinners seal deals, the host orders, toast with
  "gānbēi" 干杯 (you may sip, and tea or juice is acceptable if you say so early); respond to WeChat messages fast —
  working hours are long ("996" is common in tech); punctuality is expected of foreigners; contracts should be in
  Chinese and English with the Chinese prevailing; invoices are fapiao. Lunch is 12:00–14:00 and many offices nap.
- Work permits: a Z visa and work permit need a degree plus two years' experience as a rule; Shenzhen's "Peacock
  Plan" 孔雀计划 and talent subsidies target senior foreign experts. Companies usually handle this; verify current rules.

## Living here
- **Where expats live**: Shekou 蛇口 (Nanshan) for the international schools, Western restaurants, English-speaking
  clinics and compounds with pools — easy but a bubble; Nanshan's Houhai/Shenzhen Bay 后海 for tech workers and young
  professionals; Futian CBD/Che Kung Miao 车公庙 for the centre and the border; Luohu for cheaper, older, more local.
- **Costs** (approximate, 2026): a one-bedroom in Nanshan/Futian ¥5,000–9,000 a month, more in Shekou compounds,
  ¥3,000–4,500 further out; buying is ¥50,000–100,000 per m² centrally; eating out ¥30–60 for a local meal, ¥150–300
  for Western; metro ¥300–500 a month. Shenzhen is one of China's most
  expensive cities, still well below Hong Kong. Landlords want one month's deposit and often two months' rent up
  front; agents (Lianjia/Beike 贝壳, Ziroom 自如 for furnished flats) charge half a month's rent.
- **Health**: Hong Kong University–Shenzhen Hospital 港大深圳医院 (Futian, English-speaking, international
  department), Peking University Shenzhen Hospital, private international clinics in Shekou and Futian (United Family,
  Raffles); bring prescriptions and a supply of your own medication; pharmacies are everywhere (antibiotics
  officially need a prescription). Air quality is far better than northern China; dengue warnings in late summer.
- **Schools**: Shekou International School, QSI Shekou, International School of Nanshan Shenzhen, Shen Wai
  International School (SWIS), BASIS International, Harrow and Merchiston; English-curriculum places are costly
  (¥200,000–350,000 a year) and waitlisted — apply early.
- **Daily life**: English is limited outside Shekou and the CBD — translation apps and WeChat work; many public
  toilets have no paper; smoking is banned indoors but common outside restaurants; cameras and facial recognition are
  everywhere and normal; crime against foreigners is rare, the real hazards are traffic, e-bikes and typhoons; the
  city is clean, green (over 1,200 parks, 260 km of coastline) and very safe at night. Expect delivery drivers,
  QR codes for everything and a 24-hour convenience store on every corner.

## Food
- Local Cantonese (dim sum 点心, roast goose, congee), **Chaoshan** 潮汕 beef hotpot and beef balls (the city's
  signature, from the Teochew migrants), Hakka 客家 stuffed tofu and salt-baked chicken (the indigenous cuisine of
  the hills), plus every regional cuisine of China because everyone is from somewhere else: Hunan, Sichuan, Xi'an
  noodles, Xinjiang lamb. Seafood villages at Nan'ao 南澳 (Dapeng) and Yantian. Dongmen and Shuiwei 水围 for street
  food; Sea World and Coco Park for Western dining; Shekou for craft beer. Vegetarian and halal (清真) places exist
  but ask; "no MSG/no pork" is understood in Chinese: 不要味精 / 不要猪肉.

## What to see (a weekend)
- **Skyline and parks**: Ping An Finance Centre 平安金融中心 (599 m, Free Sky deck on 116), Lianhuashan Park 莲花山
  (Deng Xiaoping statue, view over the Civic Centre; light show on weekend evenings), Shenzhen Bay Park 深圳湾公园
  (15 km waterfront with Hong Kong across the water), Talent Park, Wutong Mountain 梧桐山 hike (944 m).
- **History**: Nantou Ancient City 南头古城 (1,700 years of the county seat, now cafés and galleries), Dapeng
  Fortress 大鹏所城 (Ming coastal garrison), Chiwan and Shekou (where the SEZ began), Shenzhen Museum (free; the
  reform-era exhibition tells the city's story).
- **Art and design**: OCT-Loft 华侨城创意园, Dafen Oil Painting Village 大芬油画村 (copies and originals, watch painters
  work), MOCAUP (Museum of Contemporary Art and Urban Planning), Shenzhen Book City, Sea World Culture and Arts
  Center (Shekou, designed by Fumihiko Maki; Design Society).
- **Theme parks** (in Overseas Chinese Town, OCT, Nanshan): Window of the World, Happy Valley, Splendid China; OCT
  East 东部华侨城 in the hills near Dameisha.
- **Beaches**: Dameisha 大梅沙 (free, crowded), Xiaomeisha, Xichong 西涌 and Jiaochangwei 较场尾 (Dapeng; B&Bs,
  surfing), plus day trips to Hong Kong, Macau (ferry from Shekou), Guangzhou (HSR) and the Kaiping diaolou.
- Shopping: Huaqiangbei for electronics, Dongmen for cheap clothes, MixC 万象城 and Coco Park for malls, Luohu
  Commercial City 罗湖商业城 at the border for tailors and copies (bargain hard).
